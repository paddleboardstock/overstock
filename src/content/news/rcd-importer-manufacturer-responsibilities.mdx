## Why the Distinction Matters

Under Directive 2013/53/EU, the Recreational Craft Directive draws a clear legal line between manufacturers and importers. Confusing these roles can result in non-compliance, product withdrawal, and liability exposure. For RIB and inflatable boat businesses selling into the EU, understanding who holds which obligation is non-negotiable.

## Manufacturer Obligations

The manufacturer is the entity that designs and produces the craft — or has it designed and produced — and markets it under their own name or trademark. Their duties include:

- **Technical file preparation** — assembling all design, test, and compliance documentation required for the applicable conformity assessment module
- **Declaration of Conformity (DoC)** — issuing the EU Declaration of Conformity confirming the boat meets all essential requirements
- **CE marking and builder plate** — affixing the CE mark, HIN, and required information on the craft
- **Owner manual** — providing documentation per ISO 10240 in the appropriate language
- **Production control** — maintaining consistent manufacturing processes that produce craft matching the assessed type

Manufacturers may be located inside or outside the EU. Their obligations do not change based on geography.

## Importer Obligations

The importer is any entity established in the EU that places a product from a third country on the EU market. Importer responsibilities are distinct and complementary:

- **Verify compliance** — confirm the manufacturer has drawn up the technical file, issued the DoC, and applied the CE mark correctly
- **Maintain a register** — keep a register of all imported craft, including model identification and import dates, for the 10-year retention period
- **Contact information on the product** — ensure the importer name and address appears on the craft or its documentation
- **Inform authorities** — cooperate with market surveillance authorities and provide the technical file upon request
- **Non-compliant products** — if an importer identifies non-compliance, they must not place the product on the market and must inform the manufacturer and authorities

## OEM Supply Chain Implications

In OEM arrangements, a factory produces boats that are sold under another brand name. The brand owner is the legal manufacturer under the RCD — not the factory. This means:

- The brand owner must hold the technical file and DoC
- The factory acts as a subcontractor with no direct RCD obligations toward end users
- The importer (if different from the brand owner) must verify that the brand owner has fulfilled manufacturer duties

ODM arrangements are similar but the factory may contribute more to design. Regardless, the entity placing the product on the market under their name bears manufacturer responsibility.

## Shared and Overlapping Duties

Both manufacturers and importers must:

- Keep documentation available for 10 years after the last unit is placed on the market
- Cooperate with national authorities during market surveillance
- Take corrective action if product risks are identified post-market

## Practical Steps for Compliance

Maintain written agreements that clearly assign manufacturer and importer roles. Ensure technical files are complete before any craft enters the EU. Register imports systematically and verify CE marking on every shipment received.
