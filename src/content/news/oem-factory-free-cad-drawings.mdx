## Why Free CAD Drawings Matter

In B2B boat procurement, engineers and naval architects need CAD files before they specify a product. If a RIB manufacturer does not provide drawings, the buyer moves to one that does. Free CAD drawings are not a cost — they are a lead generation tool. The question is not whether to provide them, but how to provide them strategically.

## Four Types of Free Drawings

### 1. General Arrangement (GA) Drawings

The foundational document showing overall layout, dimensions, and key specifications. Format: **PDF** for public access, **DXF** for engineering use. The GA gives buyers enough information to assess fit and feasibility.

### 2. Dimension Drawings

Dedicated dimensional reference sheets showing principal measurements in clean, annotation-heavy format. Format: **PDF** and **DXF**. These are the most frequently requested files — provide them for every model without requiring registration.

### 3. Deck and Installation Drawings

Showing deck layout, equipment mounting points, through-hull fitting locations, and wiring/conduit routing. Format: **PDF** for overview, **DXF** for integration. These drawings help engineers plan equipment installation and are a key decision document for project specifications.

### 4. Transport and Shipping Drawings

Showing cradle dimensions, container loading configurations, and lift points. Format: **PDF** only. Critical for logistics planning and frequently requested by distributors and fleet operators managing their own shipping.

## Three-Level Download Access

### Level 1: Open Access (No Registration)

- Dimension drawings in PDF
- GA drawings in PDF
- Transport drawings in PDF

These files have no IP risk and serve as the top of the lead funnel. Anyone can download them.

### Level 2: Registered Access (Email Required)

- Dimension drawings in DXF
- GA drawings in DXF
- Deck and installation drawings in PDF

Registration captures the buyer's identity and contact information. This is where leads enter your CRM.

### Level 3: Application Access (Company Verification)

- Deck and installation drawings in DXF
- 3D model files in STEP or IGES
- Project-specific customization drawings

Require company name, project description, and intended use. Review applications within 24 hours. This level filters competitors from genuine buyers.

## IP Protection Boundaries

Free does not mean unrestricted. Apply these protections:

- **Watermark all PDFs** — company name, "For Evaluation Only," and date
- **Strip metadata from DXF files** — remove layer names, block attributes, and author fields that reveal internal standards
- **Simplify 3D models** — STEP files for download should be faceted solids, not parametric models with design intent
- **Never share native CAD files** — no SolidWorks, Rhino, or AutoCAD source files outside the engineering team
- **Include license terms** — a simple statement that drawings are provided for evaluation only and remain manufacturer property

## Per-Model Standard Pack

For every RIB model in your catalog, maintain a standard drawing pack:

1. GA drawing (PDF + DXF)
2. Dimension drawing (PDF + DXF)
3. Deck layout drawing (PDF + DXF)
4. Transport drawing (PDF)

Update this pack with every design revision. Outdated drawings create more problems than no drawings at all.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**"Do I need to provide 3D models for free?"** No. STEP or IGES files should require application-level access. PDF and DXF are sufficient for initial evaluation.

**"What about DWG format?"** Provide DXF instead. DXF is an open format compatible with every CAD system. DWG is AutoCAD-proprietary and creates compatibility issues.

**"Should I include tube chamber details?"** No. Chamber layout, baffle positions, and valve specifications are proprietary. Show tube outline only on free drawings.
