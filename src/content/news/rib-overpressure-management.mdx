## The Thermal Expansion Problem

Air inside a RIB tube follows the gas laws. When ambient temperature rises, the internal pressure increases proportionally. A tube inflated to 240 mbar at 15°C in the morning can reach 290 mbar or more by midday in a tropical climate. This overpressure stresses seams, valves, and fabric — and can cause catastrophic failure if not managed.

## Recommended Operating Pressures

Typical RIB tube operating pressures range from 200 to 300 mbar depending on tube diameter and manufacturer specification. Key points:

- Always inflate to the pressure specified on the builder plate
- Account for ambient temperature at the time of inflation
- Never exceed the maximum rated pressure under any conditions

## Morning-to-Noon Pressure Changes

In tropical regions, a typical day cycle creates significant pressure variation:

- **Morning (6 AM)**: 20°C ambient, tube at rated pressure
- **Mid-morning (10 AM)**: 30°C ambient, pressure rises 8-10%
- **Noon (1 PM)**: 35°C ambient, pressure rises 12-15%
- **Direct sun on dark tubes**: additional 5-8% increase from radiant heating

These changes occur without any air being added. The tube pressure rises solely because the enclosed air expands.

## Boyle Pressure Relief Valves

Overpressure relief valves are essential for RIBs operating in hot climates. These valves:

- Are set to open at a predetermined pressure (typically 10-15% above rated working pressure)
- Automatically vent excess air when the threshold is reached
- Reseal when pressure drops below the threshold
- Must be inspected and tested regularly as part of routine maintenance

Not all RIBs come with relief valves as standard. Buyers operating in hot climates should specify this requirement at the time of order.

## Best Practices for Tropical Operations

- **Inflate to rated pressure in the morning** and allow the relief valve to manage midday increases
- **Do not overinflate** in the morning to compensate for expected temperature drops
- **Park the RIB in shade** when not in use to reduce radiant heating
- **Check valve function** monthly — a stuck relief valve is worse than no valve
- **Monitor pressure** with a calibrated gauge before each departure
- **Vent manually** if the relief valve is absent — open the valve briefly to release excess pressure, then reseal

## Documenting Pressure Management

For commercial and government RIBs, pressure management should be included in the standard operating procedures. Log daily pressure readings and any manual venting actions. This documentation supports safety audits and warranty claims.
