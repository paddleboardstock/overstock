## Two Documents, One Boat

The builder plate permanently affixed to a RIB and the EU Declaration of Conformity (DoC) shipped with the same boat serve different purposes — but the data they carry must be identical where they overlap. When EU market surveillance authorities inspect a vessel, they cross-reference these documents. Any discrepancy raises immediate compliance concerns and can trigger a formal investigation.

## Five Data Points That Must Match

- **Manufacturer name and address** — The entity named on the builder plate must be the same manufacturer signing the DoC. This sounds straightforward, but errors are common on OEM-produced boats where the brand owner and the factory differ.
- **Design category** — Whether A, B, C, or D, the design category stamped on the builder plate must exactly match the category declared in the DoC and supported by the technical file.
- **Maximum load** — Expressed in kilograms, this figure includes crew, personal gear, and provisions. It must be consistent across the builder plate, DoC, and owner manual.
- **Maximum number of persons** — The persons capacity on the plate must match the DoC. This number is derived from stability and buoyancy calculations per ISO 12217.
- **Maximum engine power and weight** — The power limit in kilowatts or horsepower and the engine mass in kilograms shown on the plate must align with the DoC and the technical file test data.

## Common Errors That Trigger Failures

**Counting fixed tank fuel in maximum load** — Fuel in permanently installed tanks is part of the boat's light displacement, not payload. Including it in maximum load inflates the figure and causes a mismatch with stability test data in the technical file.

**Merging CIN/HIN with builder plate data** — The Craft Identification Number (CIN), also called the Hull Identification Number (HIN), is a separate marking requirement under RCD Annex II. It must not be duplicated or conflated with builder plate fields. Some manufacturers incorrectly print the HIN on the builder plate, creating redundancy and potential mismatch.

**Using brand owner as manufacturer on OEM boats** — When a factory builds boats under another company's brand, the OEM factory is often the legal manufacturer for RCD purposes. If the builder plate lists the brand owner but the DoC is signed by the factory, the documents conflict. Both must name the same legal entity responsible for CE compliance.

## How to Verify Alignment

Create a single data sheet that feeds both the builder plate layout and the DoC template. Before any production run, cross-check every field:

1. Extract all plate values from the approved technical file
2. Map each value to the corresponding DoC field
3. Verify unit consistency (kg, kW, persons)
4. Confirm the manufacturer legal entity name is identical
5. Sign off on both documents simultaneously

## Market Surveillance Reality

EU authorities can request technical documentation within 24-48 hours of a spot check. If the builder plate and DoC disagree on even one field, the entire compliance claim comes under scrutiny. The cost of correcting a mismatch — including product recalls and re-certification — far exceeds the cost of getting it right the first time.
