## Why Safety Warnings Matter in RIB Manuals

Under the Recreational Craft Directive (2013/53/EU), every CE-marked boat must be supplied with an owner manual conforming to ISO 10240. Safety warnings are not supplementary content — they are mandatory compliance elements. A manual missing required warnings can invalidate the CE marking and expose the manufacturer to product liability claims.

## The Signal Word Hierarchy

ISO 10240 adopts a four-level signal word system aligned with ISO 3864-1:

- **DANGER** — Indicates a hazardous situation that, if not avoided, will result in death or serious injury. Used for imminent threats like explosion, carbon monoxide, or electrocution risks.
- **WARNING** — Indicates a hazardous situation that, if not avoided, could result in death or serious injury. Used for probable risks like man-overboard scenarios or tube overpressure.
- **CAUTION** — Indicates a hazardous situation that, if not avoided, could result in minor or moderate injury. Used for risks like pinch points, hot surfaces, or slippery decks.
- **NOTICE** — Addresses practices not related to personal injury, such as property damage or equipment malfunction.

Each signal word must appear in its designated color and format: DANGER in red, WARNING in orange, CAUTION in yellow, and NOTICE in blue.

## 12+ Mandatory Warning Categories

ISO 10240:2022 requires warnings covering the following topics at minimum:

1. **Tube inflation pressure** — Maximum working pressure, overpressure consequences, and cold-weather pressure increase
2. **Overpressure relief** — How pressure relief valves function and what to do if they activate
3. **Man-overboard prevention and recovery** — Specific procedures for RIBs given their low freeboard and tube interaction
4. **Kill-cord (engine safety cut-off switch)** — Mandatory use, correct attachment, and consequences of non-use
5. **Drain plugs** — Installation before launch, removal for storage, and consequences of incorrect fitment
6. **Fuel vapor and fire risk** — Fuel system inspection, ventilation requirements, and refueling procedures
7. **Carbon monoxide** — For RIBs with enclosed cabins or semi-enclosed console areas
8. **Electrical hazards** — Battery installation, circuit protection, and shore power risks
9. **Propeller strike** — Engine start procedures, swim platform warnings, and person-in-water protocols
10. **Stability and loading** — Maximum load compliance, weight distribution, and trim effects
11. **Towing and being towed** — Towing bollard load limits and correct bridle attachment
12. **Trailering** — Weight limits, tie-down procedures, and tube protection during road transport
13. **Cold weather operation** — Tube pressure management, ice loading, and fabric brittleness

## RIB-Specific Focus Areas

RIB manuals must address risks unique to inflatable collar boats:

- **Tube-to-hull joint integrity** — Inspection points for adhesive bonds and mechanical fasteners
- **Chamber isolation** — Multi-chamber design means one puncture should not disable the boat; explain baffle valve function
- **Overinflation during sun exposure** — Ambient temperature rises can increase tube pressure by 15-20% above cold-fill values
- **Reboarding over the tube** — Correct techniques using grab handles and boarding ladders

## Formatting and Placement Requirements

Warnings must appear in the relevant chapter, not consolidated in an appendix. Each warning must include the signal word, a concise hazard description, the consequence of ignoring the warning, and the required action. Pictograms from ISO 7010 and ISO 3864 should supplement text warnings where applicable.
