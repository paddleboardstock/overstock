## Why Stability Matters for Open RIBs

Open Rigid Inflatable Boats operate in some of the most demanding marine environments. Unlike cabin vessels, open RIBs rely entirely on their tube collar and hull form for stability. When seas turn rough, understanding how center of gravity (CG) and buoyancy interact becomes critical for crew safety and vessel performance.

## Center of Gravity Distribution

The CG position determines how a RIB behaves in waves. A lower CG improves initial stability but can make the boat tender in following seas. Key factors affecting CG include:

- **Engine weight** — outboard motors represent the single largest concentrated mass; mounting height and trim directly shift CG
- **Fuel tank placement** — under-deck tanks lower CG; portable tanks on deck raise it
- **Tube collar weight** — Hypalon or PVC tubes add buoyancy high on the hull, raising the effective CG
- **Payload distribution** — crew and cargo must be arranged to maintain trim within design limits

Manufacturers must declare maximum load and engine weight in the RCD technical file. Exceeding these limits raises CG and reduces the righting moment.

## Freeboard Requirements Per ISO 12217

ISO 12217 defines minimum freeboard for small craft based on design category. For open RIBs in Design Category B (force 8 and waves up to 4 meters), the standard requires:

- Sufficient down-flooding height to prevent water ingress through openings
- A minimum clear height from the waterline to any opening that could compromise buoyancy
- Verification through either calculation or physical inclining test

The tube collar contributes significantly to freeboard compliance. When tubes are partially deflated or damaged, freeboard margins shrink rapidly.

## Self-Righting Capability

Some professional RIBs are designed with self-righting capability, typically achieved through:

- **Buoyancy compartments** mounted high on the superstructure
- **Low CG** from heavy fixed ballast or deep-vee hull sections
- **Automatic righting** when the vessel inverts and high-mounted buoyancy forces a roll-back

Self-righting is not required by ISO 12217 for all RIBs, but military and rescue specifications often demand it. Testing involves a controlled capsize and measurement of recovery time without crew intervention.

## Safe Loading Practices

Stability calculations are only as good as the loading conditions they represent. Operators should follow these practices:

- Never exceed the maximum load stated on the builder plate
- Secure all cargo against shifting in heavy seas
- Distribute weight symmetrically port-to-starboard
- Reduce speed in rough conditions to minimize dynamic instability
- Monitor tube pressure regularly — under-inflated tubes reduce both buoyancy and stability margins

## Compliance Documentation

Under the RCD, stability data must appear in the technical file and the owner manual per ISO 10240. This includes the GZ curve, maximum load, and recommended CG range. Without this documentation, the vessel cannot receive CE marking.
