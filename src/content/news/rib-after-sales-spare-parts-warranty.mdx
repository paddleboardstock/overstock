## After-Sales as a Buying Decision Factor

For professional RIB buyers — government agencies, commercial operators, and fleet managers — after-sales support is often weighted as heavily as the vessel price. A boat that cannot be repaired quickly becomes a liability. Manufacturers who invest in spare parts logistics and clear warranty terms win repeat business and long-term contracts.

## Spare Parts Availability

The most critical after-sales concern is whether replacement parts can be sourced when needed. Key components include:

- **Tubes and tube sections** — partial tube replacements for damaged sections require exact fabric matching and adhesive compatibility
- **Valves** — inflation and pressure relief valves that wear or corrode over time
- **Fins and running surfaces** — sacrificial components that experience routine wear
- **Hull repair kits** — GRP patch kits or aluminum welding consumables
- **Hardware and fittings** — cleats, D-rings, lifting points, and deck hardware

Buyers expect manufacturers to commit to parts availability for a defined period — typically 10 years after the last unit of a model is sold. This aligns with the RCD technical file retention requirement and practical vessel service life.

## Warranty Scope

Warranty terms must clearly delineate what is covered and what is excluded:

### Typically Covered

- **Fabric defects** — material flaws in PVC or Hypalon tube fabric
- **Seam failures** — adhesive joint delamination under normal use conditions
- **Hull structural defects** — GRP laminate failures, osmosis, or aluminum weld defects
- **Component manufacturing defects** — faulty valves, fittings, or hardware

### Typically Excluded

- **Normal wear and tear** — abrasion, UV degradation, and cosmetic aging
- **Accidental damage** — impact, grounding, or misuse
- **Improper maintenance** — failure to follow prescribed care procedures
- **Unauthorized modifications** — non-standard repairs or alterations
- **Accessory items** — electronics, cushions, and non-structural add-ons

The warranty document must specify duration (commonly 2-5 years for fabric, 5-10 years for hull structure) and whether coverage is pro-rated.

## Claim Processing Time

Professional buyers cannot afford vessels sitting idle for weeks during warranty disputes. Expected claim processing includes:

- **Acknowledgment** within 48 hours of claim receipt
- **Assessment** within 5 business days, including inspection or photograph review
- **Resolution** — repair, replacement, or credit — within 30 days for standard claims
- **Expedited processing** for safety-critical defects that render the vessel unusable

Manufacturers who lack a structured claims process create friction that drives buyers to competitors.

## Service Documentation

Each vessel should have a service log that tracks:

- Warranty registration date and coverage period
- All warranty claims filed with resolution outcomes
- Routine maintenance records and tube pressure logs
- Replacement part installations with serial numbers
- Annual inspection results for commercial operators

This documentation protects both the buyer and manufacturer by establishing a clear service history.

## The Business Case for Strong After-Sales

Investing in after-sales infrastructure reduces total cost of ownership for buyers, increases brand loyalty, and creates a competitive moat. Manufacturers who treat warranty as a cost center rather than a relationship builder consistently lose market share to those who see it as a value differentiator.
