## Most OEM Service Pages Fail at the Same Point

They describe capabilities in vague terms — "custom solutions," "flexible manufacturing," "full customization" — without defining what those terms mean, what the process looks like, or how long it takes. The result: unqualified inquiries from buyers who are not ready, and silence from buyers who are. A well-structured OEM/ODM service page does the opposite — it educates, qualifies, and converts.

## OEM vs ODM vs Semi-Custom vs Full Custom

These four terms are not interchangeable. Define them clearly on your service page:

- **OEM (Original Equipment Manufacturer)** — You build boats to the buyer's complete design and specification. The buyer owns the design. You execute production. The buyer's brand appears on the finished product. Compliance responsibility must be contractually defined — typically shared, with the manufacturer holding the technical file and the buyer holding the brand registration.
- **ODM (Original Design Manufacturer)** — You design and build boats that the buyer rebrands. You own the design. The buyer selects from your catalog, specifies branding, and may request limited modifications. Compliance responsibility rests with the manufacturer as the design owner.
- **Semi-Custom** — The buyer starts from an existing model and modifies specific elements: console layout, seating configuration, tube color, equipment fit. The base platform is proven; modifications are scoped individually.
- **Full Custom** — The buyer specifies a boat that does not exist in your catalog. New hull design, new tube geometry, new deck layout. Requires full design, prototyping, and certification cycle.

## Detailed Customization Modules

Break down what can be customized into clear modules:

### Hull Customization

- Hull material: GRP, aluminum, or hybrid
- Hull length and beam within tooling constraints
- Chine configuration: deep-V, moderate-V, or flat-bottom
- Keel design: fixed, retractable, or shoe keel
- Color and gelcoat specification

### Tube Customization

- Fabric: PVC or Hypalon
- Tube diameter and profile: round, D-shape, or asymmetric
- Chamber count and baffle configuration
- Valve type: Halkey-Roberts, Summit, or military specification
- Color and optional graphics

### Console and Helm Customization

- Console type: stand-up, sit-down, jockey, or enclosed cabin
- Instrumentation layout and brand preference
- Steering system: hydraulic, cable, or fly-by-wire
- Windscreen type: flat, curved, or wrap-around

### Branding Customization

- Logo placement: tube, hull side, console, and transom
- Color scheme: full custom or template-based
- Builder plate and HIN: buyer's brand or dual branding
- Packaging and documentation: buyer's manual, buyer's labels

### Equipment Customization

- Navigation and communication electronics
- Safety equipment per SOLAS or local requirements
- Deck hardware: cleats, bollards, towing posts
- Specialized fittings: dive bottle racks, weapon mounts, medical equipment

## 7-Step Development Process

A clear process timeline builds confidence:

1. **Requirement Gathering** — 1-2 weeks. Detailed specification document, compliance requirements, and target market definition.
2. **Design and Engineering** — 2-4 weeks. GA drawing, structural calculations, and 3D modeling. Customer approval gate.
3. **Compliance Planning** — 1-2 weeks. Conformity assessment module selection, notified body engagement, and test plan definition.
4. **Prototype Construction** — 4-8 weeks. First article build with in-process quality inspections at each stage.
5. **Testing and Certification** — 2-4 weeks. Stability, buoyancy, and structural testing. Notified body type examination.
6. **Production Launch** — 2-4 weeks. Tooling finalization, quality control plan implementation, and first production run.
7. **Ongoing Surveillance** — continuous. Module D or E production quality assurance, annual audits, and documentation maintenance.

## Compliance Phrasing

Never write: "We can produce CE-marked boats for any market." Instead write: "All RIB models are assessed against RCD 2013/53/EU requirements. OEM projects receive compliance support including technical file compilation, notified body coordination, and DoC preparation. Compliance responsibility is defined per project in the manufacturing agreement."
