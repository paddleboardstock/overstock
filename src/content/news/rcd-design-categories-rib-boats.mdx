## What Are RCD Design Categories?

The EU Recreational Craft Directive (RCD — Directive 2013/53/EU) classifies boats into four design categories based on the sea conditions they are designed to withstand. For RIB buyers, selecting the correct category is not optional — it determines legal compliance, insurance eligibility, and operational safety.

## Category A: Ocean

Designed for extended voyages where wind force may exceed Beaufort 8 and significant wave height exceeds 4 metres. These RIB boats must survive extreme conditions with no external assistance assumed. Very few RIBs achieve Category A, and those that do require reinforced hypalon tubes, heavy-duty hulls, and advanced drainage systems.

## Category B: Offshore

Suited for offshore cruising where wind force up to Beaufort 8 and significant wave height up to 4 metres may be encountered. Most professional and commercial RIBs target Category B. Buyers should verify buoyancy calculations, stability data, and structural testing certificates.

## Category C: Coastal

For coastal waters, large bays, and estuaries with wind force up to Beaufort 6 and significant wave height up to 2 metres. This is the most common category for recreational RIBs and patrol boats operating in sheltered coastal zones.

## Category D: Sheltered

For sheltered inland waters, small bays, and calm weather with wind force up to Beaufort 4 and significant wave height up to 0.3 metres. Suitable for small tenders and lake-based RIBs only.

## Why Category Matters for Buyers

A RIB certified for Category C cannot legally be marketed for Category B use. The design category appears on the builder plate and in the EU Declaration of Conformity. Mismatched categorisation can result in:

- Product recall and market withdrawal
- Insurance claim rejection after incidents
- Liability exposure for distributors and dealers

## How to Verify the Design Category

Check the CE builder plate affixed to the boat, review the technical file, and confirm the notified body assessment covers the stated category. Request the stability and buoyancy test reports — they must demonstrate compliance at the claimed category level.

## Key Takeaway

Selecting the right RCD design category is a legal and safety decision, not a marketing preference. Always match the category to your worst-case operating environment.
