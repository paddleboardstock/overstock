## Why Hull Material Matters for Compliance

Under the Recreational Craft Directive, both aluminum and GRP (glass-reinforced plastic) RIB hulls must meet the same essential safety requirements. However, the testing methods and compliance pathways differ significantly because each material has unique failure modes and certification requirements.

## Structural Strength Testing

### Aluminum Hulls

- Weld joint strength must be verified through non-destructive testing (NDT) — typically ultrasonic or radiographic inspection
- Plate thickness and alloy specification must match the design calculations
- Fatigue testing focuses on weld-toe cracking at high-stress locations
- Material certificates per EN 573 are required for each plate and extrusion lot

### GRP Hulls

- Laminate schedule must be documented and verified against the approved design
- Layup quality is assessed through burnout testing (resin-to-glass ratio)
- Structural testing includes drop tests and impact tests per ISO 6185
- Gelcoat blister resistance testing may be required for marine-grade laminates

## Impact Resistance

### Aluminum

Aluminum hulls absorb impact energy through plastic deformation. After a significant impact, the hull may dent but typically remains watertight. Testing evaluates:

- Minimum plate thickness for the design category
- Weld integrity under impact loading
- Penetration resistance at the keel and chine

### GRP

GRP hulls resist impact through elastic deformation and laminate toughness. Testing evaluates:

- Laminate thickness and reinforcement type at impact-critical zones
- Delamination resistance under point loading
- Repair procedures for impact damage in the owner manual

## Fire Protection

This is where the two materials diverge most significantly:

- **Aluminum**: melting point around 660°C; the hull itself does not burn, but must be insulated from fuel system heat sources. Fire protection testing focuses on adjacent materials.
- **GRP**: the resin system is combustible. GRP hulls with permanently installed fuel tanks must meet fire resistance requirements per ISO 9094, including fire-retardant resin formulations where required.

## Material Certification

- **Aluminum**: mill certificates per EN 10204 Type 3.1, weld procedure qualifications, and welder certifications
- **GRP**: laminate test reports, resin and gelcoat certificates, and fibre reinforcement specifications

## Which Hull Material for Your Application?

- **Aluminum**: preferred for commercial, military, and rescue RIBs requiring durability, impact resistance, and fire safety
- **GRP**: preferred for recreational and patrol RIBs where weight, cost, and surface finish are priorities

Both materials can achieve full RCD compliance when properly engineered and tested. The choice should be driven by operational requirements, not assumptions about compliance difficulty.
