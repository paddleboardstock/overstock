## Why Airtightness Matters

For commercial RIB buyers — patrol agencies, rescue services, dive operators — tube airtightness is a safety-critical characteristic. A slow leak during an offshore operation compromises stability and endangers crew. Demonstrating airtight quality through documented testing is essential for winning commercial contracts.

## Pressure Hold Testing

The primary airtightness verification method is the pressure hold test:

- Inflate the tube to **1.25 times the rated working pressure**
- Maintain for **24 to 48 hours**
- Record pressure at start and end; allowable drop is typically no more than **5%**
- Ambient temperature must be recorded and pressure readings compensated for thermal effects

This test follows the protocols outlined in ISO 6185 and provides quantitative proof of tube integrity.

## Leak Detection Methods

When a pressure hold test shows excessive loss, the leak must be located:

- **Soap test**: apply soapy water to seams, valves, and fittings; bubbles indicate leaks
- **Backlight inspection**: in a darkened room, use a bright light inside the tube to reveal pinhole leaks through the fabric
- **Ultrasonic detection**: specialised equipment can detect the high-frequency hiss of micro-leaks
- **Water submersion**: for small components, submerge under water and watch for bubbles

## AQL Sampling per ISO 2859

For series production, testing every unit may not be practical. ISO 2859 provides acceptance quality limit (AQL) sampling plans:

- Define the **lot size** (e.g., a production batch of 50 tubes)
- Select the **inspection level** (typically Level II for normal inspection)
- Determine the **AQL** — for airtightness, an AQL of 0.65 or 1.0 is typical
- Sample the required number of tubes from the lot
- If the number of non-conforming units exceeds the acceptance number, the entire lot is rejected

This statistical approach gives buyers confidence that the production process is under control.

## Documenting Results

A complete airtightness test report should include:

- Test date and operator identification
- Tube serial number or lot reference
- Start and end pressures with temperature readings
- Duration of the test
- Leak detection findings (if any)
- Pass/fail determination and signature

Records must be retained for traceability and available for factory audit review.

## Building Buyer Confidence

Presenting structured test data with AQL sampling and clear pass/fail criteria demonstrates professionalism and quality commitment. Commercial buyers increasingly require this documentation as part of their procurement process.
