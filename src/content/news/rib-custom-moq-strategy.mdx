## Understanding MOQ in RIB Manufacturing

Minimum Order Quantity (MOQ) is the smallest number of units a manufacturer will produce for a single order. In RIB boat manufacturing, MOQ is driven by the cost of tooling, setup, and production line configuration. Understanding MOQ tiers helps new brands plan realistic entry strategies.

## MOQ Tiers

### Existing Mold: 2-5 Units

When a manufacturer has an existing hull mold and tube pattern that fits your requirements, MOQ can be as low as 2-5 units. This is the fastest and most affordable entry point:

- Use a proven hull design with your branding
- Customise tube colour, deck layout, and fittings
- No tooling investment required
- Shorter lead time (typically 30-45 days)

### Modified Existing Design: 5-10 Units

If you need changes to an existing mold — such as a different transom cutout, extended deck, or modified tube diameter — the MOQ rises to 5-10 units to amortise the modification costs.

### New Custom Mold: 200+ Units

A completely new hull design requires a custom mold, which is a significant investment:

- CNC-machined plug and mold fabrication
- Tube pattern development and prototyping
- Multiple test builds to validate the design
- MOQ of 200+ units to recover tooling investment
- Lead time of 6-12 months from concept to production

## Cost Drivers Behind MOQ

Several factors determine the minimum viable order size:

- **Mold and tooling cost**: the largest single factor for new designs
- **Material procurement**: fabric, resin, and hardware are bought in batch quantities
- **Production line setup**: each new configuration requires setup time and testing
- **Quality control overhead**: first-article inspection and test protocols for new products

## Strategies for Lower Commitment

### Mixed-Model Containers

A single 40ft container can hold 2-4 RIB boats of different sizes. Place a mixed order combining models from existing molds to reach the MOQ while offering variety in your product line.

### Shared Tooling Programs

Some manufacturers offer shared tooling arrangements where multiple brands split the cost of a new mold. This reduces the per-brand MOQ but limits exclusive design rights.

### Prototype-First Approach

Order a single prototype for market validation before committing to full production. The prototype cost is higher per unit, but it allows you to test market reception and refine the design before a larger order.

## Planning Your Launch

Start with existing molds and low MOQ to validate your brand and market. Once sales prove the concept, invest in custom tooling for your signature designs. This staged approach reduces risk and preserves capital.
