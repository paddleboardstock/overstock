## Why Payload Certification Matters

The maximum payload on a RIB builder plate is not an estimate — it is a certified value derived from calculated and tested data. Exceeding the maximum payload compromises stability, reduces freeboard, and may violate the conditions of the CE marking. Understanding how this value is determined helps buyers evaluate whether a RIB is suitable for their intended use.

## ISO 6185 Load Calculation Method

For inflatable boats and RIBs, ISO 6185 provides the primary load calculation framework:

- **Maximum number of persons**: determined by the available seating area and buoyancy capacity, with each person assumed to weigh 75 kg
- **Maximum payload**: the sum of person weight, personal equipment, and any cargo
- **Buoyancy volume test**: the total volume of inflated compartments must provide sufficient displacement to support the loaded boat at the required freeboard

The calculation accounts for the fact that inflatable compartments provide both structure and buoyancy, unlike rigid-hull boats where buoyancy comes from the hull form alone.

## ISO 12217 Stability Assessment

For RIBs, ISO 12217 provides additional stability criteria that affect the maximum payload:

- **Minimum freeboard**: the distance from the waterline to the lowest opening must meet thresholds based on design category
- **Stability angle**: the boat must resist heeling forces without capsizing
- **Downflooding height**: any opening that could flood the boat must be above the minimum height at maximum load

If increasing the payload reduces freeboard or stability below the threshold, the certified maximum must be reduced accordingly.

## Buoyancy Volume and Compartments

ISO 6185 requires that inflatable boats have a minimum number of separate buoyancy compartments:

- At least 2 compartments for boats under 4.5 m
- At least 3 compartments for boats 4.5 m and above
- At least 5 compartments for boats with motors 15 kW and above

The boat must remain afloat with any single compartment deflated. This requirement directly limits the maximum payload because the remaining compartments must support the full loaded displacement.

## Freeboard Requirements

Freeboard at maximum load must meet the design category minimums:

- **Category A/B**: higher freeboard required for offshore wave conditions
- **Category C**: moderate freeboard for coastal conditions
- **Category D**: lower freeboard acceptable for sheltered waters

## Trim Considerations

The load calculation must ensure the boat maintains acceptable trim at maximum payload. Excessive stern trim from aft loading can reduce forward freeboard and compromise handling. The owner manual should specify recommended load distribution.

## How Design Category Affects Payload

A RIB certified for Category B may have a lower maximum payload than the identical hull certified for Category C, because Category B requires higher freeboard and greater stability margins. The design category sets the performance envelope, and the payload is calculated within that envelope.
