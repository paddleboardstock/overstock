## Why Traceability Matters

Quality traceability is the ability to trace any finished RIB boat back to its raw materials, production processes, and test results. This capability is essential for product recalls, warranty analysis, and regulatory compliance. During a factory audit, the auditor will test traceability by selecting a HIN and asking to see the complete production record.

## HIN as the Traceability Key

The Hull Identification Number is the primary index for traceability. When an auditor provides a HIN, the manufacturer must be able to produce:

- The production order and build date
- The material lot numbers used in that specific boat
- The welder or operator identification for each critical process
- The test results for that unit and its production batch
- The quality inspection records at each production stage

## Material Lot Tracking

Every batch of raw material must be tracked from receipt to finished product:

- **Tube fabric**: roll number, supplier batch, test certificate reference
- **Hull material**: plate or laminate batch, mill certificate reference
- **Adhesives and sealants**: batch number, expiry date, storage condition records
- **Hardware and fittings**: purchase order, supplier certificate, incoming inspection record

When a material quality issue arises, lot tracking enables the manufacturer to identify every boat built with that material batch and take corrective action.

## Welder and Operator Identification

Critical processes must be traceable to the individual who performed them:

- **Tube welding**: each seam is logged with the welder ID and date
- **Hull-to-tube bonding**: operator ID and adhesive batch recorded
- **Final assembly**: technician ID for each major assembly step
- **Pressure testing**: operator ID and test equipment calibration record

This level of detail supports both quality accountability and warranty claim investigation.

## Test Record Linkage

Every test performed during production must be linked to the HIN:

- Pressure test results (individual compartment data)
- Airtightness test results
- Final inspection checklist
- Sea trial or water test results (if performed)

These records demonstrate that the specific boat met all quality requirements before shipment.

## Satisfying Factory Audit Requirements

Factory audits by BSCI, notified bodies, or buyer quality teams evaluate traceability by:

- Selecting a HIN and requesting the complete record
- Tracing a material lot forward to identify all affected units
- Verifying that quality records are retained for the required period (minimum 10 years for RCD technical files)
- Checking that corrective actions are documented when non-conformances are found

A robust traceability system passes audits confidently and demonstrates manufacturing maturity.

## Building Your Traceability System

Implement HIN-based tracking from the start of production, not as an afterthought. Use production management software that links material lots, operator IDs, and test results to each HIN automatically.
